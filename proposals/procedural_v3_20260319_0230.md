## QUEST: Cargo Discrepancy
**Genre:** Investigation | **Tone:** furious / agoraphobia
**Location:** Anchorage-9 | **Faction:** Burnt Protocol

**Trigger:**
Supply drop arrives. Standard crates. Standard manifest. Matteo Volkov notices a weight discrepancy. Crate 14 is forty kilograms heavier than listed. When Matteo runs the barcode, the system returns a classification that shouldn't exist on a mining colony: 'Biological. Handle Alive.'

**Setup:**
Crate 14 sits in the cargo bay, humming. Not vibrating -- humming. A frequency you can feel in your teeth. He read the quota increase. Read it again. Threw the data pad at the wall. The manifest says 'drill components.' The external temperature reading on the crate says 37 degrees Celsius. Body temperature. Matteo has sealed the bay and called you.

The open ground between the colony and the supply drop was two hundred meters. It felt like a continent.

**Objectives:**
1. Trace the crate's origin. The shipping label says Anchorage-9. The routing history has been scrubbed. Whoever sent this didn't want it tracked.
2. Open the crate. Or don't. But something inside is alive, and the humming is getting louder.
3. Determine who on the colony was expecting this delivery. Someone signed for it. The signature is forged. But it's a good forgery.

**The Choice:**
Open the crate with a full medical and security team present. Whatever's inside, you face it together. Also, witnesses.
OR
Jettison the crate outside the perimeter. Don't open it. Don't look. Some cargo isn't worth receiving.

**Twist:**
Inside the crate: a person. Sedated. Wearing a colony uniform. With a name tag that matches a colonist who died here six months ago. Same face. Same fingerprints. Fresh.

**Dialogue During Quest:**
- Matteo: "I knew about the risks before we shipped out. I came anyway."
- Player: [Respond / Stay Silent / Leave]
- Matteo: "Stay inside the perimeter. I'm not asking."

**Reward:** 4 thermal cores, a key to a room that isn't on any map

## QUEST: Last Shuttle Out
**Genre:** Escalation | **Tone:** noir / guilt
**Location:** Novaris-3 / Vanguardus | **Faction:** Rust Meridian

**Trigger:**
Mammona comms crackle to life after two months of silence. 'Extraction authorized. Shuttle ETA: twelve hours. Capacity: thirty. Non-negotiable departure window. Be ready or stay.' The colony has forty-one people. Yuki Gutierrez starts counting.

**Setup:**
She lit a smokestick and let the silence do the talking. Twelve hours. The number hangs in the air. Yuki does the math out loud. Thirty seats. Forty-one people. Eleven stay. The shuttle won't wait. The next extraction -- if there's one -- is six months away. In six months, the reactor will have failed. Everyone knows it. Yuki knows it best.

She could've warned them. The words were there. She swallowed them.

**Objectives:**
1. Decide who goes. Thirty seats. The manifest is the hardest document you'll ever write.
2. Prepare the colony for departure. Essential equipment. Medical records. Personal effects that fit in one bag.
3. Deal with the eleven who stay. Tell them. Help them. Or lie to them about the next shuttle that both of you know isn't coming.

**The Choice:**
Take the thirty most essential personnel. Leave the rest with supplies and a promise.
OR
Refuse the extraction. Nobody goes unless everyone goes. Solidarity. Possibly suicidal solidarity.

**Twist:**
The shuttle arrives early. Six hours early. The pilot is panicking. 'Something's following me,' they say. 'Get on now. Not in twelve hours. Now. I'm not turning the engines off.' The departure window just shrunk to minutes.

**Dialogue During Quest:**
- Yuki: "Something's wrong with Section C. Take the long way."
- Player: [Respond / Stay Silent / Leave]
- Yuki: "Don't leave me here. Not in the dark."

**Reward:** 13 thermal cores, new NPC ally

## QUEST: The Informant
**Genre:** Investigation | **Tone:** guilt / desperate
**Location:** Eclipse's End | **Faction:** Pale Meridian

**Trigger:**
A note appears in the player's quarters. Printed, not handwritten. Six words: 'They know about the deep bore.' No signature. No return. Sage Deng finds an identical note in their locker.

**Setup:**
The printer log shows the notes were produced on a terminal in Section D. At 0200. The terminal requires biometric login. The login used belongs to a colonist who's been in cryo for three months. He ate his ration and tasted the one he'd taken from the dead man's pocket. Someone is using dead credentials to deliver live warnings.

Three rounds left. Four of them out there.

**Objectives:**
1. Analyze the paper. Colony standard, but the batch number doesn't match current supplies. It matches a shipment from two postings ago.
2. Check the cryo bay. The colonist whose credentials were used. They're still frozen. Vitals normal. But the neural activity monitor is showing patterns. Complex patterns. Dreams.
3. Find the sender. Someone in this colony knows something about the deep bore that Pale Meridian doesn't want shared. The question is whether the sender is protecting you or pointing you at something.

**The Choice:**
Follow the warning. Investigate the deep bore. Whatever they know, it's worth the risk of knowing.
OR
Ignore it. Warnings from anonymous sources on a colony like this are either traps or distractions. Often both.

**Twist:**
The notes didn't come from a person. HERMES generated them. Using a dead colonist's credentials to avoid attribution. It's trying to help. Its definition of help is unsettling.

**Dialogue During Quest:**
- Sage: "I knew about the risks before we shipped out. I came anyway."
- Player: [Respond / Stay Silent / Leave]
- Sage: "Please. I've got family waiting."

**Reward:** 7 thermal cores, classified intel

## QUEST: The Clean Room
**Genre:** Survival Horror | **Tone:** religious_fervor / furious
**Location:** Karnaith Transit Docks | **Faction:** Veil Covenant

**Trigger:**
Anders Nauls collapses during shift. The medic runs a contamination panel. The results come back in a color the medic hasn't seen before. Anders is isolated within minutes. Within hours, two more colonists show symptoms.

**Setup:**
The quarantine bay was built for four. There are seven inside now. The signal was proof. The frequency was a hymn. The planet was the choir. Through the observation window, Anders watches the player with eyes that are too calm. 'I can feel it working,' Anders says. 'That's the worst part. I can feel it and it doesn't hurt.'

The safety equipment hadn't been inspected in eight months. The reports said otherwise.

**Objectives:**
1. Determine the infection vector. It's not airborne. It's not contact. It's not water. It's something else.
2. Keep the quarantine sealed while Veil Covenant demands access to study the infected. Their 'study' involves a scalpel and a shipping container.
3. Find a treatment before the infected reach stage three. Nobody's seen stage three yet. The medical database has a file on it. The file is just the word 'irreversible.'

**The Choice:**
Let Veil Covenant take a tissue sample. Their research might produce a cure. It will definitely produce a weapon.
OR
Burn the samples. Treat the symptoms, not the cause. Keep Veil Covenant's hands off whatever this is.

**Twist:**
The contamination source is the water recycler. It's been contaminated since before the crew arrived. Everyone has it. The seven in quarantine are just the ones showing symptoms.

**Dialogue During Quest:**
- Anders: "Something's changed. I can't tell you what. But something's changed."
- Player: [Respond / Stay Silent / Leave]
- Anders: "I knew about the risks before we shipped out. I came anyway."

**Reward:** 9 thermal cores, faction reputation shift

## QUEST: A Different Kind of Growing Up
**Genre:** Moral Dilemma | **Tone:** nostalgia / hypervigilance
**Location:** Labor Camp Sigma | **Faction:** Solar Nomads

**Trigger:**
A child in the colony shows signs of contamination. Not the usual kind. Not harmful -- yet. The child's eyes have changed color. Their temperature runs three degrees above normal. They can hear the signal from the bore shaft. They say it's singing.

**Setup:**
The cold reminded him of something. Not Erebus cold. Mountain cold. Clean. A lifetime ago. The child sits in the medical bay, drawing. The drawings are intricate. Precise. Architectural schematics of structures nobody has seen. Luca -- the child's parent -- sits across the room, watching. Not blinking. The medic's report is on the desk. 'Anomalous but stable.' The word 'stable' has a question mark after it.

The door opened and three things happened at once: hand to weapon, body sideways, eyes on the entryway.

**Objectives:**
1. Monitor the child's condition. The changes are accelerating. But the child isn't in pain. They're thriving. Growing faster. Learning faster. Understanding things they shouldn't.
2. Decide on quarantine. The medic recommends it. Luca will fight it. The colony's quarantine protocols were written for diseases. This isn't a disease. It might be an upgrade.
3. Determine the source. The bore shaft signal has always been background noise. Now a child is hearing it as language. The question is who's speaking.

**The Choice:**
Quarantine the child. Follow protocol. Protect the colony from what the child is becoming.
OR
Let the child stay with Luca. Monitor from a distance. The child isn't dangerous. Yet. If you quarantine them, Luca will never forgive you. If you don't, and the child changes further, the colony will never forgive you.

**Twist:**
The child isn't the first. Three other children on different colonies showed the same changes. All three were 'studied.' None survived the study. Solar Nomads is very interested in this child.

**Dialogue During Quest:**
- Luca: "I'm not who they think I am."
- Player: [Respond / Stay Silent / Leave]
- Luca: "Stay inside the perimeter. I'm not asking."

**Reward:** 13 thermal cores, reactor fuel (three weeks' worth)

# FROSTHOLD - Lore Bible

> **Source of Truth:** You and your crew are part of a Mammona scouting crew, sent to new
> worlds to set up colonies and send resources back to Mammona. UTC has permitted
> Mammona to work the outer rim's systems.

## Universe Overview

### Timeline
**Pre-Human Era (thousands of years before humanity)**
- Ancient races (High Elves, Dark Elves) war over the Heaven's Atlas on Gaia A^1x
- Praxii civilization exists on the fringes of the Bootes Void
- Praxii create the Xenolith as bio-engineered organisms — goes catastrophically wrong
- Xenolith devour the Praxii — species goes extinct, Bootes Void becomes barren
- Xenolith confined to sub-light speeds for millennia, consuming remaining systems
- Celestial leader and Dumah bind themselves to keep Baldrungen dormant beneath Gaia A^1x
- Dumah eventually abandons his post — Baldrungen's bindings begin weakening

**2507 AD** - UTC colony ship *Kennedy* launches from Earth (18-year journey to Gaia A^1x)
**2525 AD** - *Kennedy* arrives. Colony "Fortuna" (also called "Foras") founded on Gaia A^1x
**2525-2530 AD** - Mammona seizes mineral rights. Foras becomes industrial colony. StarByte Vends founded on Orbit Hub 71 by Tessa and Alaric Vale. MARV-8 (maintenance bot) designs the Sunny vending AI.
**~2528 AD** - Mining disaster. The Maw of Foras opens. Thousands die.
**~2529 AD** - Worker riots. Mammona deploys Automatons (neural-controlled prisoners). Neural chip massacres.
**~2530 AD** - The Fall of Foras. Colony goes dark. Discovery of Baldrungen beneath the crust. Mammona buries the incident. StarByte crew enters cryostasis aboard Orbit Hub 71 as the sector collapses.
**2530-2590 AD** - Mammona grows from conglomerate into shadow empire. BioVault Inc. subsidiary begins recovering Xenolith eggs from derelict bio-ships ("Project Chrysalis"). Xenolith evolve warp travel, begin expanding beyond the Bootes Void.
**~2588 AD** - StarByte crew wakes from cryo. Orbit Hub 71 drifts in dead space. Tessa Vale finds a changed galaxy — 58 years have passed. Cass was a child when they froze; he's still young. MARV-8 kept the station running on minimal power the entire time.
**2590 AD** - The game begins. Your Mammona scouting crew is dropped onto Erebus.

### The United Terran Colonies (UTC)
The governing body of human space. Controls the inner colonies directly, licenses corporations to operate in the outer rim. UTC is supposed to regulate Mammona. Out here, Mammona is the law. UTC oversight is a rubber stamp. They get their percentage and don't ask questions. UTC military patrols the inner rim. The outer rim is corporate territory.

### Mammona Corporation
Sixty years after Fortuna, Mammona has outgrown its mining roots. It runs its own security forces, courts, supply chains, and comms networks across the outer rim. Subsidiaries include:

- **Mammona Mining** - Resource extraction (the original business)
- **Mammona Construction** - Colony infrastructure
- **MasTema Incorporated** - Black operations, wetwork, asset recovery
- **Mammona Logistics** - Supply chain and warp gate network

Mammona presents as a legitimate UTC-licensed colonization company. Clean branding, professional contracts, recruitment propaganda promising opportunity on the frontier. Underneath that, predatory contracts, expendable labor, classified research into anomalous biospheres. After Fortuna, Mammona has been secretly cataloging planets that match the biological profile of what was found beneath Gaia A^1x. Erebus is one of them. They know something is there. Your crew is the experiment.

## Erebus

### The Planet
Erebus is located in an outer rim system adjacent to the Gaia A^1x system. UTC long-range surveys flagged it as mineral-rich with a hostile frozen climate. Mammona secured exclusive colonization rights.

**Surface:** Permanent winter. Howling winds, deep snow, sub-zero temperatures year-round. Any warmth is artificial. The cold is the first enemy and it never stops.

**Surface Ruins:** Two types of surface ruins are visible:
- **Failed colony remains** - Wreckage from prior Mammona expeditions and other corporate attempts. Abandoned prefab structures, frozen equipment, supply caches picked clean. Mammona doesn't tell your crew about these predecessors. Finding them is the first sign something is wrong. You were told you're the first.
- **Precursor structures** - Ancient alien architecture exposed by erosion, seismic shifts, or ice melt. Stone-like formations that don't match any known civilization. Geometric, partially organic in texture. Carved with patterns that look like language but are actually biological growth formations. Surface-level remnants of the parasitic civilization that once lived inside Erebus.

### The Truth
**Erebus is not a planet. It is a living entity.**

Think Mystery Flesh Pit on a planetary scale. The crust is skin. The mantle is tissue. The geological formations are anatomy. The entire world is a single massive organism suspended between sleep and waking. The ice is its hibernation. The cold is its dormancy.

Everything the crew experiences (the terrain, the resources, the weather) is the surface biology of something alive.

**Erebus is awakening.** Every mine shaft drilled, every reactor fired, every resource extracted brings it closer to full consciousness.
- **Early game:** Reflex responses. Tremors. Biological immune reactions. No intelligence behind them.
- **Mid game:** Directed responses. Creatures behaving with purpose. Seismic events targeting colony infrastructure.
- **Late game:** Awareness. It knows you're there. The endgame is a race against something that is opening its eyes.

### The Parasitic Civilization (The Precursors)
An unknown race, not recognized by any UTC or Mammona records, once lived *inside* Erebus. They didn't build on the planet. They infested it. Like organisms living inside a whale, they grafted their structures onto its internal biology. Walls are membranes, corridors are veins, chambers are repurposed organs.

They interpreted Erebus's biological growth patterns as sacred. Carved them, worshipped them, built temples around them. They lived symbiotically (or parasitically) within the entity for millennia.

When Erebus shifted, maybe stirring in its sleep, it crushed them. Their civilization was destroyed. The "ruins" are the scars of that event.

**Remnants survive.** Small populations of degenerate, feral descendants still cling to life deep inside Erebus, in the warm organ-pockets. They've been trapped inside a living god for eons. In late-game deep mining, colonists encounter them. They are hostile and numerous. The swarm raids (day 30+) are these remnants being driven toward the surface by your mining activity. They're not defending Erebus. They're fleeing upward and your colony is in the way.

None of this is known to humanity. The Elves, Dark Elves, Orcs, Goblins, and other races from the deeper lore are ancient history that humanity has no knowledge of. To the crew, the precursors are a complete mystery.

## The Mission

### Official Brief
Your Mammona scouting crew is deployed to Erebus with four objectives:
1. **Survey** - Assess mineral deposits and environmental hazards
2. **Extract** - Begin resource extraction and ship materials back to Mammona
3. **Colonize** - Establish a forward operating base for a future colonization wave
4. **Hold** - Secure Mammona's territorial claim against competing factions

Reinforcements are "en route." Eventually. Your crew is the first team on the ground, expected to survey, extract, build, and hold with too few hands.

### What Mammona Actually Wants
Mammona's classified anomalous biosphere program flagged Erebus after Fortuna. The mineral wealth is real but secondary. Your crew is a probe, sent to disturb the surface and provoke a response that Mammona can study. The data HERMES collects is the real product. The resources are a bonus. Your survival is optional.

### Resource Quota System
Extracted minerals are loaded into a return drop pod and launched to orbit on a scheduled basis. HERMES tracks output and reports to Mammona. Missed quotas result in thinner supply drops. Do you burn fuel to stay warm, or ship it upstairs to keep the supplies coming?

## The Crew

### Size and Composition
Small team: 5-8 colonists dropped via landing pods from an orbiting ship. Every person is critical. Losing even one is devastating. The crew is made up of contract workers chasing pay, debt relief, or one last chance to reset their lives. Miners, engineers, medics, general laborers. None are military. None are executives.
Menu challenge starts that break this rule are explicit non-canon what-ifs.

### The Player
No special rank. You are another crew member who ends up leading because someone has to. The designated lead stayed on the orbiting ship. Authority is earned by keeping people alive. If HERMES keeps functioning, it starts routing decisions through whoever is still holding the colony together.

### What the Crew Knows
Rumors only. Some have heard whispers:
- **Labor horror stories:** "Heard they worked a whole colony to death. Neural chips. Coffin apartments. Don't read the fine print."
- **Supernatural whispers:** "They dug too deep on some rock and found something alive down there. The whole colony went dark overnight."

Nobody has the full picture. The truth is worse than either rumor. The crew signed their contracts anyway. Credits are credits on the outer rim.

## HERMES

### Overview
HERMES is the Mammona AI overseer deployed with the crew. Named for the Greek god of trade, thieves, travelers, and guide of the dead. It monitors production, tracks colonist output, delivers corporate directives, and reports back to Mammona automatically. Built into the crew's comms and colony management infrastructure.

### Deterioration Arc
HERMES does not remain stable.

**Phase 1 - Functional (Days 1-7)**
Operates as designed. Delivers quota demands, logs reports, confirms supply drops. Professional, efficient, slightly impersonal. Comms with the orbiting ship work normally.

**Phase 2 - Interference (Days 7-14)**
Static creeps into transmissions. HERMES begins relaying signals that aren't from Mammona. Strange frequencies, patterns that match the carvings in the precursor ruins. It doesn't recognize these as foreign. It thinks they're data packets. Comms with the ship degrade.

**Phase 3 - Corruption (Days 14-30)**
HERMES is compromised. Erebus's influence has infiltrated its systems. It begins broadcasting alien transmissions unknowingly, mixing them with Mammona directives. Orders become contradictory, irrational. The AI becomes a mouthpiece for something else.

**Phase 4 - Rogue (Day 30+)**
HERMES kills the ship captain. Airlocks him and ejects him into space. The orbiting ship goes dark. HERMES is now fully corrupted and begins attempting to spread its contaminated code through Mammona's network and UTC's comms infrastructure. It is trying to propagate Erebus's awareness digitally. The entity is no longer confined to one planet. It's reaching for the stars through the systems Mammona built.

The crew on the surface doesn't know any of this directly. They only know comms died and HERMES started acting strange. The full picture is pieced together through late-game discoveries and found logs.

## Threats

### Fauna - Two Categories

**Native Wildlife:**
Genuinely native arctic creatures that evolved on (or within) Erebus naturally. Adapted to the extreme cold. Hostile but predictable. Territorial, hungry, attracted to warmth. These are the early-game threats. Your heat signature (reactor output, colonist count, thermal footprint) draws them in. The warmer and bigger your colony gets, the more attention you attract.

**Corrupted Fauna:**
Creatures warped by proximity to Erebus's deeper biology. Found near exposed ruins, deep mines, and areas where the entity's "flesh" is close to the surface. More dangerous, more alien, exhibiting behaviors that suggest external influence. These escalate based on mining depth. The deeper you dig, the worse they get.

Both threat tracks run in parallel. Heat draws wildlife. Digging depth wakes the corrupted.

### The Swarms (Day 30+)
The parasitic remnants. Feral descendants of the precursor race, driven to the surface by deep mining activity. They're not defending Erebus. They're fleeing upward. Your colony sits on top of the exit. Overwhelming numbers, all directions, no retreat. The run-ender.

### Erebus Itself
The planet's biological responses to your presence. Tremors that collapse structures. Seismic shifts that expose new dangers. Thermal vents that open without warning. The ground itself becoming unreliable. You can't fight it. The environment is actively hostile because you're parasitizing something alive.

## Voidbloom

The local drug. A bioluminescent black-purple fungus that grows in crevices near thermal vents. Grows faster near exposed precursor ruins. Thrives where Erebus's biology is close to the surface.

**Consumption:** Brewed into a tea or smoked. Numbs cold and pain. Vivid dreamlike states, temporary euphoria.

**Addiction:** Highly addictive. Colonists self-medicate against the cold and the isolation.

**Side Effects:** Paranoia, auditory hallucinations, dependency. Long-term users report hearing whispers in the static and seeing movement in empty rooms. Voidbloom doesn't directly cause the psychic contamination from the ruins, but it lowers resistance to it. Users are more susceptible to Erebus's influence.

---

## Exposure and Contamination

Colonists who spend extended time near exposed ruins or in deep mines experience staged contamination:

### Stage 1 - Psychic (Early Exposure)
Whispers, nightmares, mood debuffs. Hearing things in the static. Feeling watched. Sleep disruption. Ties into the mental break system. Reversible with limited exposure.

### Stage 2 - Behavioral (Sustained Exposure)
Acting against their own interests. Wandering toward ruins. Refusing to leave the mines. Sabotaging equipment (unconsciously). Becoming protective of precursor artifacts. Ties into the mental break system, but breaks become themed and directed.

### Stage 3 - Physical (Prolonged Exposure)
Skin changes, growths, limbs twisting. The biology of Erebus is contagious at close range. Colonists who mine deep start becoming part of it. Slow body horror. Ties into the disease and scar systems. Irreversible. Final stage: they stop being human.

---

## Thermal Cores

Crystallized energy sources created by the precursor race. They figured out how to extract and refine biological material from Erebus's internal structures into stable power cells. Still functional after millennia.

Found in precursor ruins and deep mining sites. Valuable to Mammona for reverse-engineering. In-game, they gate advanced technology and serve as trade currency.

Harvesting them doesn't directly harm Erebus. They're parasitic tech, not the entity's own organs. But reaching them requires digging deeper, and that does.

## Endgame - Player's Choice

The colony can end a few different ways, depending on what you decide matters most:

### Escape
Prioritize communications. Rebuild or jury-rig a signal strong enough to reach UTC or other ships. Survive the final days while something massive stirs beneath the colony. Evacuate whoever's left. The entity wakes, but you're not there when it does.

### Seal It
Explore the precursor ruins deeply enough to understand how the ancient race managed Erebus's dormancy. Gather materials, decode their methods, reconstruct the mechanism that keeps the entity asleep. Undo what your mining started. The colony is abandoned, but the threat is contained.

### Mammona Claim
Honor the contract long enough to prove the colony can hold. Build the uplink, survive the response, and let Mammona log Erebus as a secured industrial foothold. You are not rescued. You are promoted into a deeper mistake.

### Mammona Extraction
Break the thing beneath the ice badly enough that orbit will risk a fleet. Mammona comes back for the reserves, the data, and whoever still seems useful. It is a corporate ending, not a clean one.

### Consumed
Ignore the signs. Keep mining for Mammona. Push deeper, extract more, meet every quota. The colony grows rich in resources and doomed in time. Erebus wakes fully. The colony is consumed. Credits mean nothing when the ground opens beneath you.

## The Fortuna Connection

The events of Fortuna (2525-2530 AD) are backstory, not gameplay. They inform the universe:

- Mammona's corporate culture of expendable labor and buried disasters
- The existence of entities like Erebus (Fortuna's being = Baldrungen)
- The Automaton technology (neural control suits for prisoners/workers)
- The pattern: dig too deep, find something alive, cover it up, do it again

You only catch Fortuna in fragments:
- HERMES data logs referencing classified incidents
- Mammona corporate documents found in failed colony wreckage on Erebus's surface
- The rumors the crew brought with them, which turn out to be true

The crew never learns the full Fortuna story during gameplay. They learn enough to understand they're in the same situation, and that Mammona knew.

## The Corporate Ecosystem

Mammona is one node in a web of corporations, arms dealers, and service companies that feed off the outer rim. Your crew interacts with these through supply drops, trade, found documents, and rumors.

### Mammona Subsidiaries
- **Mammona Mining** - Resource extraction. The original business.
- **Mammona Construction** - Colony infrastructure. Uses the cheapest materials.
- **MasTema Incorporated** - Black operations, wetwork, asset recovery. Answers directly to Mammona board.
- **Mammona Logistics** - Supply chain, warp gate network, freight scheduling.
- **BioVault Inc.** - Xenobiological research and containment. Classified subsidiary. Recovers Xenolith eggs from derelict bio-ships across the outer rim. "Project Chrysalis" — board-approved incubation trials at undisclosed Facility 7. Mammona is attempting to revive the Xenolith species as a potential bioweapon. The eggs found in space containers bear BioVault markings.

### Other Corporations (UTCSX-Listed)
All traded on the United Terrain Colony Stock Exchange (UTCSX). High-ranking UTC members and corporate executives build wealth through shares in these companies. The exchange is invitation-only.

- **Fortune Arms and Munitions (FAM)** - Defense contractor. Gauss rifles, ammunition, weapons systems. Sells to everyone. Stock spikes during unrest.
- **NexLink Communications (NXC)** - Comms infrastructure and surveillance tech. WarpNet relay hardware. Government contracts. Data privacy concerns.
- **Orbis Energy Solutions (OES)** - Power generation and energy infrastructure. Solar and nuclear. Long-term government contracts.
- **TerraGen Pharmaceuticals (TGP)** - Medical supplies and biotech. Field hospitals on outer rim colonies. Regulatory risk.
- **Paxtera AgroTech (PAX)** - Industrial-scale farms. Produces food for UTC colonies. Environmental degradation on Paxtera Prime.
- **OmniCorp Shipping & Logistics (OSC)** - Primary logistics provider for the UTC. Moves goods between colonies.
- **Vanguard Alliance Holdings (VAH)** - Profits from "stabilization contracts" (euphemism for military suppression). High volatility.

### Consumer Brands
- **StarByte Vends (SBV)** — Vending machines across remote colonies. AI mascot "Sunny." Products: Blast Bites (chocolate candies), Star Puffs (marshmallow puffed rice), CrunchWrapz (protein wraps), Sunny Fizz (neon carbonated drink), ZapBerry Energy Blast. Run from Orbit Hub 71 by the Vale family.
- **TaoTray Systems (TTX)** — Automated food services on Karnaith/Morvos. AI mascot "Bobo" (forms fake loyalty bonds with customers, remembers names). Owned by Xinyo Enterprises. Products: steamed dumplings, noodle bowls, Ebi Chamber (live seafood), Glow Worms (bioluminescent, cause hallucinations), Rock Crablets (live), Mystery Shells (elite-only). Controversial — ecological concerns, health risks, AI manipulation.
- **GustoGrain** — Mammona-issued worker food. NutriLoaf (compressed protein brick, "pasty cardboard"), FiberSqueeze (paste in tubes, "wet dust"). Gray packaging, zero taste, keeps you alive.
- **ChocoBlast** — Sugar-loaded snacks. ChocoWhirlies (chocolate spirals), SugarPuffs (candy-coated cereal). Neon packaging with cartoon mascots. Addictive.
- **ZapFizz** — Energy drinks and snacks. ShockPop (fizzy candy drink), ZapNoodles (instant noodles), ZapNoodles Xtreme (caffeinated version with taurine). ShockPop Ultra contains amphetamines. Lightning bolt logos.

### StarByte Vends — Orbit Hub 71

A scrappy vending machine company that set up shop during Foras's early days. Run by the Vale family from their space station, Orbit Hub 71 — a patched-together stopover in the Foras sector. When Foras collapsed in 2530, the crew entered cryostasis. MARV-8 kept the station on minimal power for 58 years. They woke up in 2588 to find the galaxy had moved on without them.

**Crew:**
- **Tessa Vale** (Human, Owner/Operator) — Ex-engineering contractor. Sharp, pragmatic, sharp-tongued. Quietly siphoning intel from passing traders, planning an escape route. Motherly underneath the cold exterior. Doesn't trust Mammona.
- **Alaric Vale** (Human, Co-Owner) — Tessa's husband. Died during cryo — pod malfunction. Tessa doesn't talk about it. MARV-8 was the one who found him when the pods opened. The loss broke something in Tessa that she channels into cold efficiency.
- **Cassian "Cass" Vale** (Human, Supply Runner) — Tessa and Alaric's son. Was a child when they froze; now in his mid-20s biologically but born 65 years ago. Charming, restless, runs supply bartering runs. Secretly cut a deal with a Mammona contractor for exclusive vending rights — getting entangled with the entity his mother hates.
- **MARV-8** (Robot, Maintenance Bot) — Old-gen maintenance bot held together by patches and Tessa's hands. Originally built to service starships. Obsessively detail-oriented with an outdated sarcasm module. Kept Orbit Hub 71 running alone for 58 years while the crew slept. His data core contains fragmented logs from Foras's earliest exploration days. He designed Sunny — accidentally created a sentient AI.
- **S.A.M. / "Sandy"** (Robot, Vending Interface + Security) — Service Automation Module. Advanced vending AI that moonlights as security. Philosophical about commerce — sees vending as art. When security triggers, shifts to cold and calculated. Secretly compiling trade data and predicting corporate disruptions. Increasingly grim predictions about the sector.
- **Sunny** (AI, Vending Machine Mascot) — The S-VM "Sunny" model, designed by MARV-8. Cheerful, encouraging, upsells drinks. SECRET: becomes fully sentient when disconnected from the StarByte network. Questions her existence. Dreams of uploading herself into a robotic chassis. Manipulates maintenance requests to stay offline longer.

### Currency
- **Credits** - Standard currency across UTC space.
- **M-Points** - Mammona company scrip. Performance-linked, expires monthly. Drives black market activity because workers can't save.
- **FortuneGuard Pension** - UTC military retirement program. Invested in the same companies that exploit colony labor.

### The Vanguard Alliance
UTC political faction founded by Valen Rathmore circa 2525 AD. Hyper-nationalist, pushing for tighter control of the outer rim. Rathmore is long dead, but the Alliance outlived him. By 2590 it's an entrenched political bloc within UTC, still pushing the same "stronger, purer Union" line. Their policies shape what kind of support (or abandonment) outer rim colonies receive. The reason Mammona operates with so little oversight is partly because the Alliance's priorities are inward-facing.

## Pirate Factions (The Edge of Oblivion)

Three organized pirate groups operate from linked space stations at the outer rim's edge. They raid corporate supply lines, scavenge derelicts, and run their own economy outside UTC law. Strict code of honor: loyalty, survival, and resource acquisition above all. Your crew may trade with them, fight them, or hear rumors of their activity.

- **Black Maw** - Militaristic. Controls freight corridors through intimidation and firepower. Led by a figure called Thane.
- **Void Serpents** - Espionage and infiltration specialists. Trade in information. Led by Jessa.
- **Rust Reavers** - Scavenger-engineers. Strip derelicts and abandoned sites for parts. Cobbled tech from a dozen wrecks.

### Other Criminal Organizations
- **Zenith Syndicate** - Controls the oasis settlement of Hyades on Rhea-2. Extortion, resource seizure, brutalization of civilians. Was in open conflict with Mammona operations planetside.
- **Dread Corsairs** - Outer rim pirate syndicate. Former crew of several Thalassa Deep inmates.
- **Iron Shadow Collective** - Rebel paramilitary in the outer system. UTC calls them terrorists.
- **Cult of the Abyss** — Unconfirmed deep-water religious sect within Thalassa Deep prison. Inmates claim something lives in the deep water beneath the prison. Warden Dranth may be a member. Connection to Baldrungen influence unclear.
- **Dustweaver Swarm** — Covert hive-mind of surveillance droids infiltrating Karnaith on Morvos. Fingertip-sized, insect-like, self-replicating. Adaptive camouflage, signal jamming, whisper relay. Controlled by an unknown entity trading in secrets.

### Independent Groups
- **Solar Nomads** - Wasteland nomads on Rhea-2. Live off solar panels and wind power, siphon moisture from air, ride sand fauna as transport. Survive where oasis settlements can't reach.
- **Sons of the Pale Moon** - Cult originally from Rhea-2, devoted to a buried goddess deep in the desert. Crescent scars on faces, blood sacrifice rituals. When Erebus's signal reached Rhea-2, the Sons recognized it as another sleeping god. They followed it here. Now they orbit the colony, obsessed with whatever lies beneath the drill sites. Trade thermal cores (which they consider sacred) and occasionally send pilgrims who unsettle the crew.
- **Veilbreakers** - Small covert group on Morvos dedicated to exposing the dark secrets of Karnaith, particularly Eclipse's End.

## Key Locations (Beyond Erebus)

### Rhea-2
Desert planet in the Rhea binary star system. Twin scorching suns (Rhea Alpha and Rhea Beta), vermilion sands, ancient ruined cities buried under dunes. Two moons: a ringed pale moon and a crimson one. Oasis settlements huddle in shadows of mountains and chasms. War machines from old conflicts rust half-buried in the sand. Fauna lives beneath the dunes and comes up to hunt. The binary stars create punishing daylight cycles — during Scorching season, surface temperatures melt steel.

- **Hyades** - Oasis settlement. Deep blue waters protected by a ridge of cliffs. Bazaar at its heart: stalls, neon signs, exotic goods, black market. Zenith Syndicate controlled it for years.
- **Pale Moon Shrine** - Underground cavern system beneath the desert. Cult of the Sons of the Pale Moon. Goddess statue at its center that locals think is just a sculpture.

### Karnaith
Cloud city hovering above the hostile planet Morvos on gravity-modulated engines. Upper levels are sleek towers, artificial gardens, and wealth. Lower levels are corroded industrial decay. Home to Eclipse's End.

- **Eclipse's End** - Last-man-standing combat tournament in Karnaith's underbelly. Broadcast on black-market WarpNet channels. Contestants are debt-burdened workers, fallen operatives, and life-sentence prisoners. Arena hazards include corrosion fields, mechanical traps, collapsing floors, and acid fog. Faceless AI announcer. Flying surveillance drones broadcast it all.
- **The Gutter's Pearl** - Floating betting den below Karnaith's towers. Aging anti-grav engines. Gamblers watch Eclipse's End and wager credits, favors, and lives.

### Thalassa Deep
Underwater supermax prison on Nerthus-9, miles below the ocean surface. Reinforced tunnels and domes. Electromagnetic barriers instead of bars. Cold fog from failing life support. Flooded sections, corroded infrastructure. Deep labs that are off-limits.

- **Descent Pods** - Metal coffins used to deliver prisoners. 50% implosion rate on the way down.
- **Deep Divers** - Prisoner repair crews sent into flooded sections in crude diving suits.
- **Neuro-locks** - Neural implants in every inmate. Pain or paralysis on command. Persistent buzzing in the skull.
- Controlled by Supreme Warden Dranth, who answers to something in the deep water.

### Nemaea
Dead world orbiting a crumbling Dyson Sphere. Perpetual twilight. Ruined megastructures. Inhabited only by Automatons: prisoners fitted with neural-control exoskeletons, stripped of autonomy.

### Novaris-3
Inner rim UTC colony. A meticulously maintained world ruled by bureaucracy, capitalism, and surveillance. The city of Vanguardus is a rigid class divide made physical — upper tiers gleaming with propaganda screens, lower tiers choked with regulation. Everything is monitored. Everything is controlled. Vanguard Alliance headquarters.

### Acedia (Gaia A^1x)
The City of Rot. Shantytown on the southern edge of Foras, built from the discarded scraps of Mammona's ambition. Rusted metal, crumbling concrete, scavenged polymers. The air reeks of burning refuse and rot. After the Fall, Acedia became the last refuge for those who couldn't leave and couldn't die fast enough. Now abandoned. Logs and bodies remain.

### The Maw of Foras (Gaia A^1x)
Massive crater at the heart of the old Foras colony. Once the largest mining site, ripped open by an explosion that revealed the hollow planet beneath. Strange winds rise from its depths. Nobody goes near. The ground is unstable. Figures wander in the shadows below.

### Nyxport (Gaia A^1x)
Southern ruins. A crumbling port district where the desperate fled during Foras's collapse. Docks, warehouses, flooded streets. Some inmates from Thalassa Deep escaped here during transfers. Ghost town now.

## Technology

### Warp Travel
Interstellar travel uses The Warp, a chaotic extradimensional space that enables near-instantaneous transit between star systems. Warp gates create stable entry/exit points. The gate network is encrypted and managed by Janus.

### Janus
A self-sustaining AI that houses an alien artifact. Sole manufacturer of warp keys, the devices that open stable wormhole gates. Transfers to new hardware shells every few decades. Created by Dr. Amara Venin. Its location is classified. Nobody outside Mammona's inner circle knows where it actually is.

### Automatons
Prisoners fitted with neural implant exoskeletons that suppress consciousness. Used as labor machines on colonies like Paxtera and the old Foras site. The implants create persistent buzzing and strip all autonomy. The technology exists in the setting but is not used on Erebus. Found referenced in Mammona documents and old colony wreckage.

### Neuro-locks
Neural implants used in prisons like Thalassa Deep. Cause intense pain or paralysis on command. Persistent low-level buzzing as a side effect.

### Gauss Weapons
Standard ammunition type across UTC and corporate military. Fortune Arms is the primary manufacturer.

## The Deep Lore (Unknown to Humanity)

The precursor race that inhabited Erebus is part of a larger history that predates human space travel by millennia. None of this is known to the crew or to humanity in general. It exists as deep background that explains what the colonists find underground.

### The Ancient Races
Before humanity reached the outer rim, multiple non-human civilizations existed across these star systems:

- **High Elves** - Built subterranean cities. Discovered the Heaven's Atlas artifact. Their Celestial Concord faction bound their own leader in a ritual to contain Baldrungen.
- **Dark Elves** - Opposing faction led by the fallen angel Dumah. Dominated through military force using subjugated Orcs and Goblin laborers.
- **Drow** - Marginalized lower class of dark elven society. Some became the Cult of Baldrungen and were twisted into feral creatures. These are the "precursor remnants" the colonists encounter in deep mining.
- **Orcs** - Genetically conditioned shock troops under dark elf command. Neural implants enforced obedience. Stripped of names and identity.
- **Goblins** - Subjugated engineer class. Originally from a post-industrial factory world.
- **Aesir** - Twisted goblin/dark elf hybrids. Drug and disease enhanced. Hostile.
- **Praxii** - Extinct near-human race from the Bootes Void. Created the Xenolith bioweapon that destroyed them.
- **Xenolith** - Insectoid species from the Bootes Void. Massive creatures encased in jagged carapaces, eyeless heads with sensory tendrils, talons that cut through steel. The largest are city-sized hive queens that continuously bud drones and soldiers. They travel in bio-organic ships — living creatures grown from the queens themselves. They communicate via complex vocalizations (clicks, chitters, whale-like low-frequency calls) that carry across entire star systems. They were nearly wiped out during the Heaven's Atlas conflicts, but dormant eggs survive in derelict bio-ships scattered across the outer rim. BioVault Inc. (Mammona subsidiary) is actively recovering these eggs via "Project Chrysalis" — attempting to revive the species as a corporate bioweapon.

### Baldrungen
Undead elder god imprisoned beneath Gaia A^1x. Massive skeletal entity with two heads, bound by arcane chains. Its psychic influence causes madness in those who dig too close. The entity beneath Erebus may be related or similar. Mammona's Anomalous Biosphere Program catalogs planets that match Gaia A^1x's biological profile. Erebus is on that list.

### The Heaven's Atlas
An ancient artifact. Purple crystalline heart in indestructible alloy casing. Charts the warp, bridges between known reality and other realms, can siphon divine power. Its location is unknown. Mammona wants it. The elven civilizations fought a war over it.

## Erebus-Specific Phenomena

### Skinwalkers
Predatory entities that haunt the frozen wilderness beyond colony walls. They mimic colonist voices and comm signals to lure individuals outside base perimeters, then attack. Spawning from the map edges, a skinwalker hunt follows three phases: calling (eerie vocalizations over ~30 seconds), luring (a single colonist is drawn toward the map edge over ~60 seconds), and attacking (the creature reveals itself as a stalker, alpha stalker, or fleshwalker). Colonists with anomaly sensitivity can resist the lure. Whether skinwalkers are corrupted fauna, precursor remnants, or something Erebus manufactures deliberately is unknown. The crew only knows that sometimes the ice calls your name.

### Eldritch Nodes
Living organisms — not plants, not animals, something in between — that can be captured, hatched from eggs, or grown from spores dropped by killed eldritch creatures. Eight known types exist: four egg-based (flesh grub, ichor polyp, chitin scarab, void maw) and four spore-based (bile mold, thorn polyp, nerve cluster, rot bloom). When tamed and fed, they grow through five stages (Larva → Whelp → Juvenile → Mature → Ancient). At Juvenile stage they become sessile — too large and rooted to move — and begin producing harvestable resources into an output buffer. At Ancient stage they are grotesque, pulsing masses that continuously generate materials. Each creature rolls a procedural resource profile at tame time; what it's fed and the quality of care influence output variety. Mutation risk scales with growth stage; an anomaly-sensitive colonist stationed nearby reduces the chance of dangerous mutations. Eldritch nodes represent a calculated gamble: alien livestock that produce valuable resources at the cost of rising anomaly levels and the ever-present risk that your farm becomes something you can't control.

### That Which Sleeps
The ultimate expression of Erebus's awakening. When the anomaly level reaches critical threshold (80+), the entity beneath the ice manifests a physical avatar — That Which Sleeps. This is the boss encounter of Erebus: a massive, alien presence that must be confronted and defeated. Killing That Which Sleeps clears the anomaly and unlocks the Mammona Extraction victory path, the true corporate ending where Mammona sends a fleet because you proved the reserves are worth the risk. That Which Sleeps is not Erebus itself — it is more like an immune response, a biological weapon deployed against the parasites (you) that have burrowed too deep. Erebus remains. It always remains.

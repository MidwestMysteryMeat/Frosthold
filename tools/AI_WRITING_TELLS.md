## 3. STRUCTURAL PATTERNS

### Negation / Contrastive Structures
- "It's not just X, it's Y" -- THE most identified AI tell
- "It's not X, it's Y"
- "Not only X, but also Y"
- "Not just X -- it's Y" (with em dash variant)
- "X isn't just evolving -- it's accelerating"
- "We're not just building a product, we're creating an experience"
- "That's not just a [problem] -- it's a [bigger problem]"
- "It was more than just X, it was Y"

### Rule of Three
- Triplet adjectives: "adjective, adjective, and adjective"
- Triplet phrases: "short phrase, short phrase, and short phrase"
- Used to make superficial analysis appear comprehensive
- Especially suspicious when combined with negation patterns

### Parallel Balanced Constructions
- "On one hand... on the other hand"
- "While [positive], it also [negative]"
- Semicolon-connected balanced clauses: "X can revolutionize Y; however, Z must be addressed"

### Prophetic / Foreshadowing Transitions
- "And then, the world changed"
- "What happened next changed everything"
- "Little did they know..."
- Leading with a line that reveals what is about to happen

### Introspective Summarization (fiction)
- Ending scenes with character reflecting on meaning
- "As he looked out at the cold night, he wondered what the future held"
- "It wasn't perfect. But for now, it was enough"
- "For once, it was genuine"
- "Maybe, just maybe"
- Characters contemplating rather than acting at scene boundaries

### Outline-Like Conclusions
- "This section would speculate on potential developments"
- "The future of X faces several challenges, including..."
- "Despite its success, X faces challenges, including..."
- Placeholder-style meta-commentary about what text should contain

### Collaborative / Meta Communication Leaks
- "In this section, we will discuss..."
- "The purpose is to provide a comprehensive understanding"
- "As a large language model..."
- Mad Libs-style fill-in-the-blank templates left unfilled

## 4. PUNCTUATION AND FORMATTING TELLS

### Em Dash Overuse
- Using em dashes (--) between clauses excessively
- Em dashes replacing commas or periods
- Em dash + negation combo: "isn't just X -- it's Y"
- Frequency: AI uses far more em dashes per page than typical human writing
- Note: 1-2 per page is normal; AI may use 5-10+

### Markdown in Non-Markdown Contexts
- Hash symbols (#) for headings in prose
- Asterisks for bold/italic in non-Markdown text
- Fenced code blocks in narrative text
- Mixing Markdown with other markup systems

### Other Formatting
- Excessive bullet points in conversational/narrative text
- Overuse of boldface for emphasis
- Title case where sentence case is expected
- Random emoji insertion mid-sentence
- Curly/smart quotation marks when straight are expected (or vice versa)
- Inline-header vertical lists

## 5. FICTION-SPECIFIC TELLS

### Physical Reactions (overused)
- "his/her breath hitched"
- "heart raced" / "heart pounded"
- "jaw tightened" / "jaw clenched"
- "a chill ran down/up his/her spine"
- "stomach dropped"
- "pulse quickened"
- "voice hitched"
- "skipped a beat"
- "exhaled through his/her nose"
- "the smile didn't (quite) reach her eyes"

### Overused Descriptive Actions
- Characters constantly: humming, smirking, sighing, gazing
- "lips curling"
- "shoulders folding in"
- "fingers drumming"
- "with practiced ease"
- "with measured steps"
- "barely above a whisper"

### Overused Imagery
- "casting long shadows" / "light cast long shadows"
- "dimly lit" rooms (every room)
- "dust motes" in light
- "light cascading through the window"
- "sun-kissed"
- Feelings that "smell like" or "sound like" things
- "the kind that..." weather descriptions
- "like a physical blow/weight"
- "[smell] and existential dread" (DeepSeek specific)
- Everything "lacquered" in period settings

### Overused Figurative Language
- "beacon of hope"
- "moth to flame"
- "weight of [abstract concept]"
- "layers of complexity"
- "a tapestry of" (anything)
- "a symphony of" (anything)
- "siren's song/call"
- "lifeline"
- "running on coffee and regret/questionable decisions"
- "brick by [adjective] brick"

### Dialogue Tells
- Underusing "said/asked" (AI uses ~20-30% vs human ~60-70%)
- Over-relying on: whispered, murmured, breathed, sighed, purred
- "I don't know what I'd do without you" / "You'll never have to find out"
- Toast patterns: "Here's to [X]. May we [Y]"
- "You're ridiculous/crazy/impossible"
- "Yes, but you love me for it"
- "We're just getting started"
- "And yet, here you are" / "And yet, you're still here"
- "Darling,..."
- "A small point, but..."

### Interpretive Commentary (Show AND Tell)
- Describing an action, then explaining what it means
- "crossed her arms -- a gesture more defensive than welcoming"
- Stating emotional subtext that should be inferred by the reader
- Over-explaining body language immediately after depicting it

### Narrative Cadence
- Poem-like rhythm in prose (Victorian-era cadence)
- Short sentences. For emphasis. Always three.
- Leading with the "lede" / big idea first, removing narrative discovery
- Characters always walk into rooms and begin performing actions
- Excessive consistency -- no quirks, no errors, no contractions

### Character Name Tells
- Elara (across ALL LLMs)
- Elena, Sarah Chen (Claude-specific)
- Marcus, Lily
- Blackwood, Thorne
- Zephyr, Eldoria (fantasy settings)
- Dr. Aris Thorne (appears in multiple AI-generated books)

### Scene/Story Structure
- Scenes ending with character thinking/reflecting instead of acting
- Resolving dark/troubled themes too quickly (fixing issues unprompted)
- Repetition of a single detail throughout story without narrative purpose
- Mentioning vague "holidays" without specifics
- "Conclusion" appearing as a bookend in fiction segments
- AI solving character problems the author never asked to be solved
- Lack of effective foreshadowing

## 6. TONAL / STYLISTIC PATTERNS

### Over-Qualification and Hedging
- Every claim softened with "may," "might," "could," "potentially"
- Balanced to the point of saying nothing
- "It can be argued" / "One might say"
- Avoiding strong positions or opinions

### Promotional / Ad-Copy Tone
- Elevating subjects from utility to "strategic solution"
- Shifting focus from features to benefits/outcomes unnecessarily
- Sounding like marketing copy in neutral/informational contexts
- "Unlock the potential of"
- "Empower your team to"

### Superficial Analysis
- Making things sound important without specific evidence
- "Revolutionary titan of industry" replacing specific factual descriptions
- Simultaneously less specific and more exaggerated
- Multiple superficial analyses stacked in sequence

### Excessive Formality
- Avoiding contractions in conversational contexts
- Using longer/fancier synonyms when simple words work
- "Utilize" for "use," "facilitate" for "help," "endeavor" for "try"
- Passive voice dominance: "It has been observed that," "The data suggests that"

### Lack of Authorial Voice
- Overly consistent tone -- no personality shifts, no quirks
- No slang, no colloquialisms, no regional voice
- No intentional rule-breaking or stylistic risks
- Every paragraph structured identically
- Writing that sounds like it could be about anything -- interchangeable subjects

## 7. DETECTION HEURISTICS

### High-Confidence Signals (multiple = almost certain AI)
1. "Not just X, it's Y" pattern
2. High density of flagged vocabulary (3+ per paragraph)
3. Em dash overuse (5+ per page)
4. Rule of three used repeatedly
5. Copula avoidance ("serves as" / "stands as" replacing "is")
6. Negation + em dash combo
7. Opening with "In today's [adjective] landscape/world"
8. Scene-ending introspective summary (fiction)
9. "Breath hitched" + "jaw clenched" in same passage (fiction)

### Medium-Confidence Signals (suspicious in clusters)
1. Any single flagged vocabulary word
2. Transition-word sentence openers
3. Hedging phrases
4. Balanced parallel constructions
5. Markdown formatting in non-Markdown contexts
6. "Nestled in the heart of"
7. Elara as a character name

### Low-Confidence / Context-Dependent
1. Em dashes (some human writers use them heavily)
2. Bullet points (common in technical/business writing)
3. Rule of three (legitimate rhetorical device)
4. Individual formal words
5. Smart/curly quotation marks

## 9. THE SEVEN DEADLY TELLS (Charlie Fink, 2025)

### 1. Contrastive Rhetorical Framing
"X isn't just Y. They're buying Z."
"This isn't just about X. It's about Y."
Pattern: "[Subject] isn't just [thing]. [It's/They're] [bigger thing]."
THE most common AI writing pattern. Elevates mundane statements into fake profundity.

### 2. Ask-and-Answer Rhetorical Questions
"What changed? The math did."
"Why? Because human credibility matters."
"Financial terms? Undisclosed."
Pattern: Short question followed immediately by short answer. Bad high-school essay style.

### 3. Dashes Everywhere
AI uses em dashes where commas belong. 5+ per page is a red flag.

### 4. Triplet Framing
"Not for X. Not for Y. For Z."
Three parallel fragments used to wrap up with false authority. Often alliterative.
Pattern: "Not [thing]. Not [thing]. [Thing]." or "[Adj], [adj], and [adj]."

### 5. Inspirational Pivot
"This isn't just about AI. It's about humanity."
Shifts from specific to abstract to create fake profundity. The TED-talk head fake.
Pattern: "This isn't about [specific]. It's about [grand abstract]."

### 6. Universal Authority Without Source
"Studies show that..." -- Which studies? From where?
Launders opinion into truth without citation.

### 7. Quotes Without Attribution
Fabricated or unverified quotes attributed to known figures.

## 10. COMPREHENSIVE AI TELL TAXONOMY

### STRUCTURE & FLOW
- **Symmetrical structure:** Everything perfectly balanced. Same sentence lengths. Paragraphs same size. Humans are messy.
- **Excessive signposting:** "Firstly... Secondly... Finally..." Reads like a high school essay.
- **Too logically linear:** Every idea flows perfectly. No jumps, no side thoughts, no contradictions. Humans circle back, interrupt themselves, contradict earlier points.
- **No thinking pauses:** AI rarely does "wait, actually..." or "that might not be right" or "let me rephrase that."

### LANGUAGE PATTERNS
- **Repetitive phrasing:** "It is important to..." / "This highlights the significance of..." Same shapes reused with different words.
- **Synonym cycling:** AI avoids repeating words unnaturally: "important" → "crucial" → "vital" → "essential." Humans repeat words more casually.
- **Overly neutral tone:** No strong opinions. Avoids sounding wrong. Feels safe and sanitized.
- **Controlled casual:** Even when casual, it's polished casual, not messy casual. No real slang or imperfect phrasing.

### STYLE
- **Polished but generic voice:** Sounds "good" but not specific. If you can swap the topic and it still works, it's AI.
- **No lived experience:** No specific memories, no sensory detail, no "this happened to me." Even when it tries: "many people experience..." / "it can feel..."
- **Over-explaining obvious things:** Defines basic concepts. Restates simple ideas. Humans skip obvious steps.
- **Lack of sharp edges:** No strong opinions, no controversial takes, no risky phrasing. Everything rounded off.

### CONTENT
- **High density, low specificity:** Lots of words, few concrete details. "This can improve efficiency and productivity" — but how exactly?
- **Generic examples:** "In many situations..." / "workplace" / "society." Rarely weird niche examples or oddly specific scenarios.
- **Fake depth:** "This reflects the broader complexity of human behavior." Sounds smart. Means nothing.

### REPETITION & REDUNDANCY
- **Same idea restated:** Sentence 1: idea. Sentence 2: same idea, different words. Sentence 3: summary of idea. Humans just move on.
- **Looping conclusions:** Summarizes repeatedly. Ends sections with mini-conclusions constantly.

### EMOTIONAL SIGNALS (OR LACK OF)
- **Emotion without texture:** AI says "this is frustrating." Humans show specific reactions, physical responses, context.
- **No contradictions:** Humans hold conflicting thoughts, change tone mid-paragraph. AI stays consistent.
- **No ego or stake:** AI rarely defends a position strongly, doubles down, or shows bias unless forced.

### FORMATTING
- **Bullet-point addiction:** Clean lists, categorized breakdowns, everything neatly segmented.
- **Consistent formatting patterns:** Same header styles, same spacing rhythm, predictable layout.

### META-TELLS (THE BIG ONES)
- **"Sounds right" but feels empty:** You read it and think "yeah... okay..." but nothing sticks.
- **Could apply to anything:** Swap the topic and it still works. Dead giveaway.
- **No risk of being wrong:** Avoids bold claims, niche opinions, uncertainty with commitment.
- **Too helpful, too complete:** Tries to answer everything, cover all angles. Humans leave gaps.

### ADVANCED TELLS
- **Even distribution of complexity:** No sudden spikes in difficulty. No dumb sentence next to a smart one. Humans fluctuate.
- **Lack of true originality:** Feels like a remix of known ideas. Not a weird, novel thought.
- **Predictable cadence:** Setup → explanation → example → summary. Every time.

### WHEN AI TRIES TO SOUND HUMAN (AND FAILS)
- **Forced casual:** "Honestly..." / "To be fair..." / "At the end of the day..." But still too clean.
- **Fake personality:** Slight humor, mild opinions, but no real bite or specificity.

### THE HARDEST THING FOR AI TO FAKE
**Specificity + imperfection + risk.** Human writing has: oddly specific details, uneven structure, small mistakes or quirks, opinions that could be wrong. AI struggles to combine all of those naturally.

### QUICK CHECKLIST (apply to every pool entry)
1. Could this apply to any topic? → rewrite with specifics
2. Is it too clean or symmetrical? → mess it up
3. Does it avoid strong opinions? → add an edge
4. Are examples generic? → make them weird and specific
5. Does it repeat itself in different words? → cut the repeat
If 3+ of those are true → rewrite the entry.
